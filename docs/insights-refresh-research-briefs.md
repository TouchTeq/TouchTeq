# Touch Teqniques Engineering Insights Refresh

## Research briefs and copy-ready prompts

## Purpose

Touch Teqniques Engineering is refreshing its five existing Insights articles so that they match the editorial quality of the Industrial Instrument Calibration guide. The goal is not merely to decorate the old copy. Each article should be freshly researched, fact-checked, restructured and then redesigned around the questions industrial decision-makers actually need answered.

The refreshed articles should:

- serve plant managers, maintenance teams, reliability teams, process-safety practitioners and engineers in South Africa and the wider Southern African service region;
- distinguish legal requirements, standards requirements, accepted engineering practice and company recommendations;
- cite authoritative, current sources next to the claims they support;
- explain technical concepts in practical language without oversimplifying them;
- help the reader make a decision, prepare a scope or identify a gap;
- lead naturally to a relevant Touch Teqniques service without turning the article into a sales brochure; and
- use the calibration guide's visual rhythm while retaining a subject-specific identity.

## Recommended research and publishing order

1. **IEC 61511 for plant managers** — highest priority because standards-edition, legal-accountability and functional-safety claims can change or be misinterpreted.
2. **Hazardous area classification in Southern Africa** — high priority because it contains regulatory, certification, mining and country-specific claims.
3. **Fire and gas system commissioning** — closely connected to Touch Teqniques' core service and strong commercial intent.
4. **SIL assessment vs HAZOP** — an important educational comparison that can reuse verified functional-safety sources from the IEC 61511 research.
5. **Optical flame-detector false alarms** — useful and practical, but it needs manufacturer-specific evidence and careful wording because behaviour varies by detector technology and model.

Complete and approve one research report before starting the next article redesign. This creates a repeatable standard and avoids five simultaneous rewrites with inconsistent evidence.

## Evidence standard for every research report

### Source hierarchy

Prioritise sources in this order:

1. Current legislation, regulations, government departments and official gazettes.
2. Official standards-body pages and legally accessible standards guidance, including IEC, SABS and ISO.
3. Accreditation, certification and professional bodies acting within their documented mandate.
4. Original equipment manufacturer manuals, application notes and technical bulletins.
5. Peer-reviewed papers, recognised engineering institutions and established technical publishers.
6. High-quality secondary sources only when a primary source is unavailable, clearly labelled as secondary.

Do not use an unattributed blog, distributor sales page, AI-generated summary or search-result snippet as the sole support for a technical, legal or compliance claim.

### Claim rules

- Verify the current edition, amendment status and publication date of every named standard.
- Do not reproduce paywalled standards text or invent clause wording. Use official summaries and clearly state when the full standard was not accessible.
- Separate **South African legal requirement**, **standard requirement**, **recognised engineering practice** and **recommended site practice**.
- Do not say that a standard is legally mandatory unless an exact legal instrument or binding contractual requirement supports that statement.
- Do not imply that Touch Teqniques holds an accreditation, certification, approval or service scope unless exact company evidence is supplied.
- Do not generalise one manufacturer's instructions to all devices.
- Preserve uncertainty. Mark a claim as **not confirmed** when the available evidence cannot support it.
- Use exact dates rather than words such as “currently” when describing editions, regulations or programmes.
- Flag any claim that needs review by a qualified engineer, legal specialist, authorised inspection authority or company owner before publication.

### Required research-report structure

Return the report in this order:

1. Executive summary in plain language.
2. Intended audience and the decision the article should help them make.
3. Search intent and five to ten realistic reader questions.
4. Current South African context, with Southern African differences clearly separated.
5. Verified technical explanation.
6. Practical process or workflow.
7. Five common client misconceptions, each followed by a concise evidence-based correction.
8. Five FAQs with direct, source-supported answers.
9. A “What not to promise” section covering legal, compliance, accreditation, certification, performance and service-scope boundaries.
10. Plant-manager or engineering-team checklist.
11. Recommended article outline with proposed H2 and H3 headings.
12. Suggested tables, diagrams, callouts and image concepts.
13. A claim ledger using the table below.
14. Contradictions, uncertainties and claims requiring expert confirmation.
15. Full source list with direct links, publisher, document title, publication or revision date, access date and source type.

Write in a technical, clear and trustworthy tone. Do not use sales language, hype, filler or unsupported certainty. Never invent or imply accreditation, certification, approval, legal authority, guaranteed compliance or a company capability that has not been confirmed with exact evidence.

Use this claim-ledger format:

| Proposed claim | Status | Source and direct link | Source date | Geographic scope | Publication wording or caution |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Example claim | Verified / Partly verified / Not confirmed | Official source | Date | South Africa / International / Manufacturer-specific | Exact limitation |

Every important factual claim in the recommended outline must be traceable to the ledger.

## Prompt 1: IEC 61511 for South African plant managers

Copy and use the complete prompt below.

```text
You are conducting source-led technical and regulatory research for Touch Teqniques Engineering, a South African industrial engineering company serving plant, maintenance, reliability, process-safety and engineering teams.

We are refreshing an existing article provisionally titled “IEC 61511 in South Africa: What Plant Managers Need to Know.” Produce a detailed research report that can support a new, practical, visually structured Insights article. Do not write marketing copy and do not merely paraphrase the existing article.

Research objective:
Help a South African plant manager understand what IEC 61511 covers, what decisions and responsibilities sit with the operating organisation, how the safety lifecycle affects an operating plant, and what evidence should exist before the organisation claims its functional-safety arrangements are adequate.

Research these questions:
1. What is the latest confirmed edition and amendment status of IEC 61511 Parts 1, 2 and 3 as of the research date? Verify this on official IEC pages.
2. What does IEC 61511 apply to, and how does it distinguish a safety instrumented system, safety instrumented function and safety integrity level?
3. What are the main phases and management obligations in the functional-safety lifecycle?
4. What should plant managers understand about functional safety management, competence, independence, verification, validation, functional safety assessment, proof testing, bypasses, management of change and lifecycle records?
5. How should SIL be explained accurately for low-demand, high-demand and continuous modes without using misleading universal “10-fold/100-fold” shortcuts?
6. What is the exact, current relationship between IEC 61511 and South African legislation or regulation, including the Occupational Health and Safety Act and Major Hazard Installation Regulations? Separate direct legal wording from accepted engineering practice. Do not state that IEC 61511 is legally mandatory unless an exact authoritative source proves it.
7. Which South African duty-holder, employer, operator, competent-person or approved-inspection-authority concepts are actually defined in authoritative sources, and which should not be attributed to IEC 61511?
8. What has genuinely changed in the latest edition or amendment? Specifically verify any claims about cybersecurity, competence and failure-rate data. If a claimed “2026 edition” cannot be confirmed on an official IEC source, say so explicitly.
9. What documents and records should a plant manager ask to see when reviewing a SIS programme?
10. What practical warning signs suggest that the documented SIS and the plant's actual operating state have drifted apart?

Source requirements:
- Prioritise official IEC catalogue and publication pages, South African legislation and government sources, official SABS information, and recognised functional-safety institutions.
- Use secondary technical commentary only to explain context and label it clearly.
- Do not quote or reproduce protected standards text beyond short, legally permissible excerpts.
- Provide a direct link for every source.

Editorial and boundary requirements:
- List five common client misconceptions and provide a concise evidence-based correction for each.
- Provide five FAQs with clear, source-supported answers suitable for the end of the article.
- Include a “What not to promise” section covering legal, compliance, accreditation, certification, performance and service-scope boundaries relevant to this topic.
- Use a technical, clear and trustworthy tone. Do not use sales language, hype, filler or fluff.
- Do not invent or imply Touch Teqniques accreditation, certification, legal authority, guaranteed compliance or an unconfirmed capability.

Required output:
Follow the Evidence standard and Required research-report structure supplied with this prompt. Include a claim ledger, uncertainties, an expert-review list, and a source-backed proposed article outline. Recommend at least one lifecycle diagram, one “documents to request” checklist, one misconception callout and one practical management table.
```

## Editorial design standard for the later rebuild

The calibration article should be the foundation, not a rigid template. Each refreshed article should include:

- the established image-led hero and consistent metadata row;
- Share and audio-summary controls in the same position;
- an “In this guide” navigation panel on desktop, with a compact mobile equivalent;
- a strong opening answer or decision statement;
- shorter text sections separated by diagrams, comparison tables, checklists, numbered workflows and caution cards;
- descriptive headings that let a reader scan the argument;
- a clearly labelled source and further-reading section with direct links;
- an authority/review box that states only a genuine review date and reviewer;
- related-article links forming a useful topic cluster; and
- one service CTA that matches the reader's likely next step.

Do not turn every paragraph into a card. The visual hierarchy should reduce fatigue: prose establishes context, while designed elements are reserved for comparisons, decisions, warnings, workflows and practical takeaways.

## Suggested topic-cluster links

- **IEC 61511** should link to **SIL vs HAZOP**, **fire and gas commissioning**, and relevant functional-safety services.
- **SIL vs HAZOP** should link back to **IEC 61511** and forward to commissioning or assessment services.
- **Fire and gas commissioning** should link to **flame-detector false alarms**, **hazardous area classification**, and commissioning services.
- **Flame-detector false alarms** should link to commissioning, maintenance and detector-related services.
- **Hazardous area classification** should link to commissioning wherever equipment certification, installation readiness or classified-area testing is discussed.
